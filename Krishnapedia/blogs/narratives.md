# Narrative Bias

As a student of statistics, I get cautious about bias. Not just quantitative bias, but also qualitative forms of bias that can be found in the narratives we tell.

One of my friends told me a horrendous narrative about a common acquaintance, and I immediately asked, “What is their narrative about this incident?” Me asking this question often made my friends question my loyalty towards them :p, but now they understand. 

I find it difficult to believe that this acquaintance holds this horrendous narrative about themselves and sleeps peacefully without guilt. 

Even if this narrative is true, I suspect they would rationalise or delude themselves into building another narrative that gives them peace.

There is literature on rationalisation that deals with this, but I also speak from personal experience. 

I’m not a vegan, but I’m convinced that the “colour of milk is red.” For a vegan, my food habits are horrendous, but I didn’t hold that narrative. I rationalised my food habits (often in the name of science) to sleep peacefully.

We do the same elsewhere too. 

We have the power to rationalise our worst habits in the name of ideology, science, religion, history, nationalism, and whatnot. And we produce a wide range of narratives about individuals and communities in that process. This has been happening since the dawn of human civilisation.

The alternative is probably living with guilt and changing, of course. But I’m not sure about the forces that facilitate it.
