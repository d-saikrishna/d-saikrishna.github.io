# Tools for Conviviality
Ivan Illich articulated an interesting concept called “convivial tool” in this book: [Tools for Conviviality](https://aakarbooks.com/product/tools-for-conviviality/)
<div style="text-align: center"><img src="https://aakarbooks.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Tools-for-Conviviality.jpg" style="width: 50%; max-width: 500px;"/></div>


A convivial tool is something that liberates the individual and doesn’t make them dependent on it or external conditions. A bicycle is a very good example to illustrate it.

This is how we can decide if a tool is convivial or not:
1. We can repair them, decide how to use them and control them.
2. ⁠They can be used by anyone. There might be a learning curve though. But there will not be any artificial restrictions.
3. ⁠They are gentle on nature. Atleast during its operations, they should not demand much from environment and society.

Free and open source software (FOSS) movement meets these criteria well too.

Illich says traditional libraries also were convivial. But today’s big libraries have become complex and bureaucratic - failing on the second criterion.

There are many other interesting ideas in the book, but its a tough read. Small is Beautiful by Scumacher does better with similar ideas.

Question for you: Is data center a convivial tool?
